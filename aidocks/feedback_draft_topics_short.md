When the dev asks for a **draft** of a help topic (or any doc) to review before the final, write only **2-3 short paragraphs** — a sample that shows the direction, voice, and how a tricky section will read — NOT the whole finished topic.

**Why:** the dev reviews by screen reader; a full-length "draft" is the whole deliverable and defeats the point of a quick review pass. Confirmed 2026-08 on the tile-zone topic — the full draft content was fine ("perfect"), but it was too long for a draft; "a draft means a short page or 2, not the whole thing."

**How to apply:** a draft is purely the CONCEPT and VOICE — what the element IS and how it behaves — in 2-3 short paragraphs (e.g. the intro plus the one comparison/section the dev flagged). Deliberately leave OUT how it's built or edited (form/menu/keys) AND what the individual fields do — those belong only in the final (dev-confirmed 2026-08: "right to the point, nothing about how it can be built or edited, and nothing about what the fields do"). Save all of that mechanical detail for the full write-out after they approve the direction. The final topic itself still follows [[feedback_tp_prose]] and the house structure.
