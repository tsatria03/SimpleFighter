Staircases.

A staircase is a platform that always spans a range of tiles and is climbed through rather than simply stood on — it's how you connect a lower area to a higher one without making the player jump. Where a plain platform sits at a single height, a staircase covers a stretch the player walks up: crossing it carries them smoothly up (or down, coming the other way) as a slope instead of a flat floor. It is one of the platform forms and shares their whole surface model, so the platforms topic is worth reading alongside this one.

The surface.

Everything about a staircase's surface works exactly like a plain platform. It takes the same tile chosen from the tile sound list, the same tile volume and tile pitch, the same health, and the same destroyable and overlap options, and the tile list previews its step, land, and fall clips on space, control-L, and control-H the same way. A staircase sounds like whatever surface you paint it with — a stone staircase steps like stone, a wooden one like wood. The platforms topic explains each of these fields in full, along with the special air tile.

The shape.

The only thing that sets a staircase apart from a plain platform is its shape. A staircase always takes a minimum and maximum x and a minimum and maximum y, even on 2d maps, because the range it spans is the whole point — that span is the run of the stairs the player climbs across. On 3d maps it also takes a minimum and maximum z, and it fills every height level between them so the staircase becomes a solid ramp the player ascends as they cross its depth, carrying them from the bottom z up to the top z.

Use a staircase wherever the player needs to change height as part of walking — between floors of a building, up a hillside, onto a raised platform — and reach for a plain platform when the ground is flat.

Staircases versus slants.

A staircase and a slant both carry the player up or down as they walk, and they overlap in purpose, but they differ in feel. A staircase is a platform with a real footing tile the whole way up, so it sounds like the surface you paint it with and can be made destroyable. A slant is a lighter ramp with its own topic, and unlike a staircase it's limited to 2d and 3d maps. Reach for whichever fits the surface you want.

Authoring staircases by hand.

If you edit main.sif directly, a staircase is: staircase minx maxx miny maxy health tile volume pitch destroyable overlap — note that, unlike a plain platform, it always carries both a min and max y even on 2d maps. On 3d maps a min and max z follow the y pair: staircase minx maxx miny maxy minz maxz health tile volume pitch destroyable overlap. The fields after the coordinates match a plain platform's exactly: volume is a number from zero down to minus one hundred, pitch is a percentage around one hundred, and destroyable and overlap are the literal words true or false. The tile must match a tile folder name or the word air.
