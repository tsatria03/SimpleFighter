Sonars.

The sonar is an audio scanning tool that reads the map around you and pings back what it finds - walls, hazards, staircases, vanishing platforms, solid ground, and open gaps - as a positioned sound, so you can hear the shape of a room and what lies ahead without walking into it. It is one of the main ways to feel out a map on foot.

Turning it on and the scan modes.

The sonar has three scan modes, and you step through them with the sonar mode key (shift plus f2 by default): off, step, and loop.

Off: the sonar is silent and scans nothing.
Step: the sonar sends one ping each time you take a step, reading the direction it is aimed as you move. Stand still and it stays quiet; walk and it reports with every tile you cross.
Loop: the sonar pings on its own on a repeating timer, whether or not you are moving, so you get a steady stream of readings. The timing is set by the speed control below.

Cycling the mode is how you switch the sonar on and off - move from off to step or loop to start it, and back to off to stop it.

Scan speed.

The speed key (shift plus f3 by default) cycles the loop's ping rate through slow, medium, fast, and super fast. It only matters in loop mode, where it sets how often the automatic pings fire: a slow rate gives you room to listen to each one, a fast rate paints a quicker, busier picture. Step mode ignores the speed, since it fires on your steps instead.

How the sonar is aimed, and the follow modes.

The follow key (shift plus f1 by default) cycles how the sonar decides which way to scan:

Follows you: the sonar always scans in the direction you are facing, turning with you as you turn. This is the everyday mode - look somewhere and the sonar reads that way.
Does not follow you: the sonar holds a fixed direction that you aim by hand with the sonar direction keys (j, l, k, and i for left, right, back, and front, plus shift plus i and shift plus k to aim up and down on a 3d map). It keeps scanning that direction until you point it elsewhere, which is handy for watching one side of you while you move another way.
Both directions at once: the sonar scans left and right on the same beat, with the left ping pitched a little lower than the right so you can tell them apart, giving you a read on both sides without choosing one.

The sonar direction keys share their physical keys with the camera markers; when you are in camera mode the marker behavior takes over, and otherwise the same press aims the sonar. See the controls topic for the exact bindings and how to rebind them.

What the sonar reports.

Each scan reaches out in its direction and reports the nearest thing it meets, played from that spot so you hear both which way it is and roughly how far. Every kind of feature has its own sound:

A wall, so you know where a barrier or the edge of a room is.
A hazard, warning you of something dangerous that way.
A staircase, with a different sound depending on whether the stairs lead up or down from where you are standing.
A vanishing platform, marking ground that comes and goes.
Solid ground, a floor tile you can safely stand on.
Open air, a gap with no floor - the cue that there is nothing to walk onto that way.

Sonar while jumping.

When you jump, the sonar looks straight down to find the floor beneath you and pings it, so you can hear where you are going to land. On a 3d map it searches down through the levels below you for the first solid ground.

Sonar on 3d maps.

On a 3d map, facing forward or backward scans away from and toward you across the floor, and the up and down direction keys (shift plus i and shift plus k) scan a level higher or lower so you can check what is above or below you. Everything else works the same as on a flat map.

Sounds.

Every sonar cue is drawn from your character's own map sound set, so different characters can ping with different sonar sounds. There is a separate clip for walls, hazards, stairs up, stairs down, vanishing platforms, solid tiles, and open air. See the customizing audio section of the readme for the clip names.

The arena.

The sonar is turned off inside the arcade arena, so its keys do nothing there.
