Gliders.

A glider is a weapon class that turns into a portable flight rig the moment you draw it. There is no separate glider entity type and no info-file field to set — the glider behavior is keyed off the weapon's folder name. Any weapon whose folder name in equipments/weapons/<category>/<name>/ starts with the literal word glider is a glider; glider, glider2, glider_fast, glider_silent all count, while a melee weapon that happens to be named paraglider does not. The check is on the leading characters only, not anywhere in the name.

Engine control.

The glider has an engine that you turn on and off with the Ctrl key. Holding Ctrl turns the engine on — the on clip plays once, the engine loop starts looping, gravity stops pulling you down, and the player's normal movement keys begin moving you freely without needing a platform underneath. Engaging the engine also lifts you up by one altitude above your current spot, the same takeoff bump an aircraft gives when it leaves the ground — engaging from the ground at altitude 0 puts you at 1, and engaging mid-fall at altitude 11 puts you at 12.
Releasing Ctrl turns the engine off — the loop stops, the off clip plays once, and gravity resumes immediately. If the engine was on while you were airborne, releasing it drops you under standard fall physics; if you re-press Ctrl before you hit the floor, the engine catches you at your current altitude the same way the grappling hook catches you when you re-grab. This is the safety valve: careless mid-flight releases are punished, deliberate ones are not. Drawing a different weapon mid-flight also disengages the engine, plays the off clip from the glider's pack, and drops you under standard physics; if you re-equip the glider mid-fall and press Ctrl, the catch logic engages again.

If you descend back below altitude 1 while the engine is running — usually by holding the height-down key into the ground — the glider auto-lands you instead of droning at ground level: the tile's land clip plays, the engine's off clip plays, the engine cuts out, and you're set on the tile beneath you. A hand glider isn't a jet — it touches down rather than crashing. Holding Ctrl through the auto-land stays silent, as it does through a stamina-empty cutoff: the engine won't re-engage while you're still pressing Ctrl, so to take off again you release Ctrl (clearing the latch) and re-press it. This stops the engine re-engaging the moment you touch ground, which would otherwise loop you between landing and lifting off.

Stamina fuel.

If the glider's info file sets a non-zero stamina drain (and optionally a health drain), the engine consumes that each tick it runs, the same per-tick cadence a normal weapon's drains use. A glider with stamina drain 10 and standard stamina gives roughly a full stamina pool of flight — enough for a real crossing, not enough to fly all day. The moment stamina hits zero mid-flight the engine cuts out, the off clip plays once, and you fall under standard physics from your current altitude. Holding Ctrl through the fuel-out stays silent — the engine won't re-engage on its own as regen refills the pool, since rapid on/off cycling would be useless noise. To take off again, release Ctrl and re-press it once stamina is above zero.

A glider with stamina drain 0 and health drain 0 is the original free-flight model — no fuel limit, fly indefinitely on a single Ctrl-hold — which is still the right choice for a pure movement-aid glider. Authors who want to limit flight time, charge a cost for crossing hazard fields, or build a high-risk high-power glider that bleeds health to stay aloft set the drain fields accordingly.

Movement.

While the engine is on, the player's existing movement keys move at the player's existing cadence — arrow keys for x and y, height step keys (page up / page down or W / S on 3d maps) for altitude, the run modifier (Alt) for the faster cadence, body rotation keys for facing, all unchanged. There are no new keybindings to learn; the glider just changes the semantics of those keys so movement no longer requires a platform underneath. All other collision still applies — walls block, map edges clamp, doors block when closed, blockages block, hazards on the ground don't hurt you because you're aloft above them but the wall standing in your path still bumps you the way it bumps a vehicle.
Jumping is blocked while the engine is on, since flight makes it redundant. Tile step sounds are suppressed during flight too — the engine loop is the only audio cue for movement. Both behaviors resume the moment you release Ctrl and the engine cuts out.

Combat.

The glider is a melee weapon underneath, but it strikes on collision, not a fire button. The strike fires when the tile beneath the glider meets a hittable entity's tile — and since takeoff lift puts the glider one altitude up, flying over a ground-level entity counts as contact. It repeats at your movement cadence (one per walktime walking, one per runtime running) as long as you stay on the entity's tile, so resting on a target keeps hurting it; step off and it stops. Each strike deals the weapon's damage, plays its hit clip and the entity's hurt cue (pausing the entity's taunt or loop so the cue lands clean), and logs "N damage to <entity> by <weapon>'s weapon attack." If it kills the entity, the weapon's death clip plays instead.
The same entities the cloner copies can all be hit this way (see the cloners topic for the full list). Calendars, clocks, signs, and items are removed in a single strike, as bullets and melee already break them; the rest lose health over multiple strikes until zero. Entities with destroyable false are skipped, as a normal weapon skips them; vehicles, bikes, and aircraft are skipped while driven or piloted, since only parked ones take damage. Things weapons cannot normally hurt — zones, audio entities, switches, hazards, mines, force fields, fires, time bombs, moving hazards, vanishing hazards, character or command blockers, checkpoints, spawnpoints, travelpoints — are not affected either.

Bumping a wall while the engine is on plays a glider-specific rico clip from the weapon's pack instead of the wall's own bump sound. The wall's bump audio is suppressed during flight the same way tile step audio is, so the only collision sound you hear is the glider's ricochet. The wall still blocks the move, the same way it would block a vehicle, so you just don't pass through it; you also don't damage the wall by ramming it (use the glider's contact strike on a destructible wall column from on top of it instead).

Invincibility while flying.

While the engine is on, the player is immune to every damage source in the game — the same protection a transport vehicle gives you when you're inside it. Hazards, force fields, spikes, bombs, mines, fires, and time bombs all skip you entirely if you fly over or onto them. NPC bullets pass through you, NPC projectiles miss, NPC melee strikes don't connect, TTS enemy attacks don't fire at you, and the NPC AI's combat-target picker treats you as nonexistent for both provocation and combat selection.
The moment you release Ctrl and gravity takes over, every damage path reopens immediately — so a glider that flies you across a hazard field is safe in the air but lethal the second the engine cuts and you drop onto the spikes below. Heal zones and other non-damaging effects (safe zones, info zones) still apply normally so you can patch yourself up or trigger map narrative while flying. Fall damage still applies when you land from a fall — the invincibility is engine-only, not a blanket effect of the glider being drawn.

Sounds the glider's pack must provide.

The glider expects six audio clips in its general folder, each playing in a specific situation:

draw.ogg — the standard weapon-draw clip, plays once when you equip the glider, the same as any other weapon's draw.
on.ogg — plays once when you press Ctrl and the engine starts. Should sound like an engine turning over or wings opening.
loop.ogg — loops continuously while the engine is on, stops the moment you release Ctrl. The "running engine" sound.
off.ogg — plays once when the engine stops (whether from releasing Ctrl, drawing a different weapon, or any other engine-stop path). Should sound like an engine spooling down or wings folding.
hit.ogg — plays on every successful contact strike. Same role the hit clip plays on a normal melee weapon.
death.ogg — plays on the killing blow when a contact strike drops an entity's health to zero or destroys a single-use entity.
rico.ogg — plays when the glider bumps into a wall while the engine is on. This replaces the wall's own bump clip during flight.

Authoring a glider.

Create a new folder under sounds/decompiled/main/equipments/weapons/<category>/ whose name starts with glider. Drop the seven clips above into the folder's general subfolder. Add a data/info.sif with the same fields any weapon uses, but most are forced or ignored for gliders: fire mode (forced to 1), bullet speed (ignored), repeat time (ignored), spam time (ignored), ammo and loaded ammo and max ammo (ignored), launch type (ignored), ammo type (forced to none), x range and y range and z range (ignored — contact is tile-exact, not range-based), melee (forced to true), scadder (ignored), swing speed (forced to 0), weight (forced to 1), reflection (forced to false), refchance and refcushion (ignored), life drain (forced to 0).

The fields that do matter are damage (how much each contact strike deals), unlock level (the character level needed to equip it), stun mode (whether the strike stuns), push x, push y, and push z (whether contact also knocks the target tile-wise), health drain (whether each strike costs your own health), and stamina drain (whether each strike costs stamina). The shop's per-ammo-type damage upgrades and the max ammo upgrade do nothing here, since the glider's ammo type is none and it has no ammo. The melee damage upgrade does apply, since the glider is internally a melee weapon, so investing in melee damage scales its contact strikes like any other melee weapon.

Practical use cases.

A low-damage glider is a movement aid first and a weapon second: useful for scaling buildings without the grappling hook, exploring tall maps without staircases, or skipping over hazard fields you cannot otherwise cross. A high-damage glider becomes a flying brawler — fly into an NPC and the per-cadence strike chips away at it while you stay aloft, repositioning between flurries by gliding back out of contact and circling around. Pairing a glider with a low-cost or zero-cost damage value gives you a non-violent scout rig; pairing it with high health drain turns it into a risk-reward weapon where every hit costs the wielder.

Gliders show up in the weapons menu exactly the way any other weapon does — you can equip them, swap between them, and they obey unlock level gates against the player's current level. The character can carry the glider in either of the character-file weapon type fields if you want a starting kit that comes pre-equipped with one.

Things the glider deliberately does not do.

The glider has no flight timer — engagement is purely Ctrl-up versus Ctrl-down (with the stamina-fuel cutoff if the weapon sets a stamina drain, above). It doesn't refill on draw, use the reload key or ammo readout (it has no ammo), or use the reflection toggle (the T key does nothing, reflection forced off). It doesn't chain hits — each contact tick makes one strike against the first matched entity at your tile, even if several share it. It doesn't pass through walls, doors, blockages, or any movement-blocking entity; it removes only the gravity requirement, not the rest of the collision system. And it doesn't auto-engage on draw — you press Ctrl every time you want to fly, even if you were flying when you switched away.
