Containers.

A container is a roaming trap that hunts the player down, scoops them up, and drops them from a height. It rolls around the map homing in on the player, and when it reaches them it catches them, carries them high into the air, holds them there a moment, and then lets go — so the fall is what does the damage. A container never hurts the player directly; the whole danger is the drop at the end. The player's goal is to destroy it with their weapons while it is still rolling, before it ever gets hold of them. Containers only exist on 2d and 3d maps, since a topdown map has no height to drop from.

How a container works.

A container rolls on a single tile, looping its rolling sound so the player can hear where it is, and homes toward the player along whichever axes it is allowed to move on, one tile every interval. Its seeing range is a live leash: it only wakes and chases while the player is within range, gives up and halts where it stands if they outrun it past that range, and picks the chase back up if they return. It passes straight through walls, so the way to escape a rolling container is to outrun it or destroy it, not to duck behind cover.

When a rolling container reaches the player's tile it catches them — but only if they are on foot; it cannot grab a player who is flying a glider, driving a vehicle, or riding a bike. Once caught, the player is held fast: they cannot walk, but they can still turn and fire their weapon. The container carries them upward to its drop height, hovers there for its hover time, and then drops them, releasing them to fall the way they would off any ledge. A tall enough drop wounds or kills; a container set to a low drop height gives a gentler fall. After it drops the player it removes itself, so each container catches only once.

Destroying a container.

A container has health and can be destroyed by the player's weapons, both bullets and melee, the same as any other breakable object — it sounds a hurt cue with each hit and a death cue when its health runs out. Destroying it while it is still rolling is the safe play, since a destroyed container can never lift the player. If the player is already caught they can still fire while trapped, and every shot lands on the container around them; destroying it in mid-air releases the player to fall from wherever it had carried them, so a quick trigger finger can turn a full drop into a short one. Bringing a container down rewards the player with experience, scaled by the container's level, and announces the defeat in the combat log.

Container fields.

The container form takes the following:

Coordinates. An x and a y (and a z on 3d maps) — the tile the container starts on.

X seeing range. How far along the x axis the container can notice the player. Defaults to the map's full width.
Y seeing range. How far along the y axis the container can notice the player. Defaults to the map's full depth.
Z seeing range. On 3d maps, how far along the height axis the container can notice the player. Defaults to the map's full height.

Speed. The number of milliseconds between each step the container takes, both while rolling toward the player and while carrying them upward, so a smaller number is a faster, more aggressive container.

Health. How much damage the container can take before it is destroyed. Defaults to 1.

Level. The container's level, used to scale the experience the player earns for destroying it.
XP. The base experience awarded for destroying the container, multiplied by its level and the player's experience rate. Set this to zero for a container that gives no reward.

Drop height. How high the container carries the player before letting go, given as a height on the map. It cannot be lower than 8, and defaults to the map's full height. A lower drop height means a shorter, less damaging fall.

Hover time. The number of milliseconds the container holds the player at the top before dropping them. Defaults to 2500.

Container sound (a folder name from the container sounds list, or none). The audio theme; the bundle supplies the rolling loop, the catch, flight, hover, and drop cues, the removal cue, and the hurt and death cues. Pick none for a silent container.

Move on x axis (checkbox). When checked, the container moves toward the player along the x axis once it notices them.
Move on y axis (checkbox). When checked, the container moves toward the player along the y axis once it notices them.
Move on z axis (checkbox). On 3d maps, when checked, the container moves toward the player through height once it notices them.

Auditioning sounds in the builder.

While the container sound list is focused in the form, press space to hear the highlighted theme's hurt clip, and hold control and press: L for the rolling loop, N for the spawn cue, C for the catch cue, I for the flight loop, O for the hover cue, D for the drop cue, R for the removal cue, and H for the death cue.

Authoring containers by hand.

If you edit main.sif directly, a container on a 2d map is written as container x y xrange yrange speed health level xp dropheight hovertime containertype movex movey, where movex and movey are the literal words true or false. On a 3d map a z follows the y, a z range follows the y range, and a third move flag follows the others: container x y z xrange yrange zrange speed health level xp dropheight hovertime containertype movex movey movez. The ranges, speed, health, level, xp, drop height, and hover time are numbers, and the container sound must match a container sounds folder name or the word none.
