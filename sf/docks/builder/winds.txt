Winds.

A wind is a trap that hunts you like a fire, but where a fire kills the instant it touches you, a wind knocks you around instead. Once you come within its sight it chases you across the map, and when it catches you it shoves you a set number of tiles in the direction it blows, into a wall but never through it, and, if it was given any health, wears a little off you with each hit. Leave its health at zero and it becomes a harmless gust that only pushes. A wind is the trap for a gale that drags you toward a ledge, a gust that keeps knocking you off your path, or a battering wind that both shoves you and hurts.

How a wind works.

A wind blows from a single tile, looping its wind sound from that spot so you can hear where it is. Each time the wind reaches your tile it strikes: it pushes you the set number of tiles in the direction it blows, and if its health is one or more it also plays its hit sound and takes that much of your health. The push stops at the first wall in its path, so a wind can pin you against a wall but never shove you through one, and being blown off the edge of a platform lets you fall as normal. A wind with its health left at zero never makes a sound when it reaches you and never hurts you; it only pushes. Because the damage flows off your health like any other, a wind that keeps catching you can wear you all the way down and kill you.

How hard a wind presses you comes from its speed. Speed sets both how often the wind steps toward you while chasing and how often it strikes, so the two go together: a fast wind hounds you and hammers away several times over, while a slow wind drifts up now and then and only buffets you once in a while. This is why a wind's push is always at least one tile, so each strike carries you clear and the wind has to come back for the next one rather than sitting on top of you.

A wind also has a seeing range on each axis and a set of move-axis switches, exactly like a fire. While you stay outside the seeing range the wind sits where it was placed. The moment you step within range on every axis it notices you and, from then on, homes toward you along whichever axes it's allowed to move on, advancing one tile at a time. Once a wind has noticed you it stays locked on, so the only ways to escape a moving wind are to outrun it or to break its path with a wall or other obstacle. A wind with no move axes switched on never travels; it's a stationary gust that only acts when you walk onto it. On 3d maps a wind can chase you through height as well.

Wind fields.

The wind form takes the following:

Coordinates. An x and a y (and a z on 3d maps) — the tile the wind blows from.

X seeing range. How far along the x axis the wind can notice you. Defaults to the map's full width.
Y seeing range. How far along the y axis the wind can notice you. Defaults to the map's full depth.
Z seeing range. On 3d maps, how far along the height axis the wind can notice you. Defaults to the map's full height.

Speed. The number of milliseconds between the wind's strikes, which is also how often it steps toward you while chasing, so a smaller number is a faster, harder-hitting wind.

Push distance. How many tiles the wind shoves you each time it catches you, along the direction it blows. The push stops at the first wall in the way, so you're never pushed through solid ground. It must be at least one.

Health. How much of your health the wind takes each time it reaches you. Leave it at zero for a wind that only pushes and stays silent when it hits; set it to one or more and the wind plays its hit sound and takes that much health with every strike.

Wind sound (a folder name from the wind sounds list, or none). The audio theme; the bundle supplies the blowing loop and the hit clip played when the wind catches you. Pick none for a silent wind.

Direction. Which way the wind blows and pushes you, chosen from a list. On a 2d map the choices are left, right, down, and up; on a topdown map they are left, right, backward, and forward; on a 3d map all six are offered, with down and up moving you through height.

Move on x axis (checkbox). When checked, the wind moves toward you along the x axis once it notices you.
Move on y axis (checkbox). When checked, the wind moves toward you along the y axis once it notices you.
Move on z axis (checkbox). On 3d maps, when checked, the wind moves toward you through height once it notices you.

Auditioning sounds in the builder.

While the wind sound list is focused in the form, press space to hear the highlighted theme's hit clip, and hold control and press L to hear its blowing loop.

Authoring winds by hand.

If you edit main.sif directly, a wind on a 2d or topdown map is written as wind x y xrange yrange speed push health direction windtype movex movey, where movex and movey are the literal words true or false. On a 3d map a z follows the y, a z range follows the y range, and a third move flag follows the others: wind x y z xrange yrange zrange speed push health direction windtype movex movey movez. The ranges, speed, push, and health are numbers, and the wind sound must match a wind sounds folder name or the word none. Direction is a number that sets which way the wind blows: 1 is left and 2 is right on every map; 3 and 4 are down and up on a 2d map, or backward and forward on a topdown or 3d map; and on a 3d map 5 and 6 add down and up through height.
