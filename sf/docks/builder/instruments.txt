Instruments.

An instrument is a tile the player stands on and plays like a keyboard, tapping out melodies, chords, and drums on the letter and number keys. It's a toy for making music inside a map: place one in a quiet corner, a stage, a music room, or anywhere you want the player to be able to sit down and play. It makes no sound on its own until someone opens it and starts pressing keys.

An instrument is built in two variants, chosen from the build mode list: a single instrument (one tile) and a ranged instrument (a rectangular area the player can play from anywhere inside).

How an instrument works.

Stand on an instrument and press the interact key to open it. It says "Instrument opened" along with the category and instrument it's currently set to, such as "pianos, classical grand", and from then on the letter and number keys play notes instead of moving you. The rest of the game is paused while you play, so nothing wanders up and hurts you and your keys don't do anything but make music. Press escape to close it; it says "Instrument closed" and hands control back to the game exactly where you left off. Everything you change while playing, the category, the chosen instrument, the pedals, is remembered by that instrument until you close the map, so you can step away and come back to the same setup.

Playing an instrument.

The keys are laid out in four rows. The number row plays the lowest notes, the top letter row the next, then the home row, then the bottom letter row for the highest notes, giving you thirty-six notes in all. Not every instrument fills all thirty-six; where a note isn't provided, that key is simply silent.

Press Tab to cycle through the three instrument categories: pianos, drums, and others. Pianos and others are melodic; drums are a kit. Press the Left and Right arrow keys to step through the instruments within the current category, and it names each one as you land on it. Each category remembers its own last-used instrument, so switching categories and back returns you to what you were playing.

Hold the Up or Down arrow key to bend the pitch of the notes that are ringing, sliding it up or down for as long as you hold the key; let go and the pitch snaps back to normal.

Press Enter to turn the overlap pedal on or off. With overlap off, each new note cuts the one before it, so the instrument plays one note at a time like a melody. With overlap on, notes ring together, so you can build up chords by pressing several keys.

Press Backslash to turn the staccato pedal on or off. With staccato on, a note stops the instant you release its key, so held keys sustain and quick taps come out short and clipped. With staccato off, every note rings out to its full length no matter how briefly you tap.

Recording a performance.

An instrument can record what you play and play it back, so you can lay down a phrase and hear it again. Press Alt plus R to start recording; it counts down three, two, one, gives a beep, and says "recording", after which everything you play is captured, including how long each note is held and any pitch bends you make. Press Alt plus Return to stop recording. Press Alt plus P to play the recording back; while it plays, press Return or R to stop it early. A recording lives only until you close the map or record a new one over it; there is no way to save it to a file or load one back, on purpose, so recording is for playing something back in the moment rather than building a library.

Instrument fields.

The instrument form takes only its position:

Coordinates. A single instrument takes an x and y (and z on 3d maps); a ranged instrument takes a minimum and maximum x and y (and a minimum and maximum z on 3d maps), chosen with the ranged option in the build mode list.

There are no other fields. The category and instrument aren't set in the builder; they're chosen live while playing, and an instrument can't be destroyed.

Authoring instruments by hand.

If you edit main.sif directly, a single instrument is: instrument x y. On 3d maps a z follows y: instrument x y z. A ranged instrument replaces the single tile with minimum and maximum pairs: instrument minx maxx miny maxy, inserting a minimum and maximum z after the y pair on 3d maps: instrument minx maxx miny maxy minz maxz. The line is nothing but the word instrument and its coordinates.
