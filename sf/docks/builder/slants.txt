Slants.

A slant is a ramp that carries the player up or down as they walk across it. Instead of pressing jump to gain height, the player climbs a slant by simply walking along it: every tile they move carries them up, or down the other way, by a set amount. Slants are available on 2d and 3d maps only, since a topdown map has no vertical axis to climb, and the builder hides the entry on topdown maps.

How a slant works.

A slant is a horizontal footprint of floor with one low end and one high end. You set where its low end sits and how steeply it rises, and the ramp lays a run of floor that climbs from the low end to the high end. As the player walks toward the high end they rise a step at a time; walking back toward the low end lowers them the same way. On 2d maps the ramp changes the player's height as they move left and right. On 3d maps it changes their height as they move along whichever direction the ramp faces.

A ramp is always a two way slope: whichever way climbs, the opposite way descends. There is no separate down ramp to build. You name the ramp by the way that goes up, and walking the other way brings the player back down on its own.

The direction you pick reads as a plain instruction, walk this way to go up. On 2d maps the choices are left up, left down, right up, and right down. On 3d maps forward up, forward down, backward up, and backward down are added. Because a ramp can be named from either end, some choices describe the same physical slope from opposite ends, so pick whichever matches how you picture walking it.

How a slant differs from a staircase.

A staircase and a slant both change the player's height as they walk, without a jump, so it helps to know which to reach for. A staircase spans a whole block of tiles the player climbs through, rising one level per tile with no choice of steepness or facing; it's the quick way to connect a lower area to a higher one. A slant is a ramp you shape: you pick which way it climbs and how steep it is with the slope amount, and it lays a single sloped floor with a landing at the top. Reach for a staircase when you just need to get from one height to another, and a slant when you want a ramp of a particular direction and steepness, such as a gentle driveway or a steep incline.

Slant fields.

The slant form takes the following:

Coordinates. A minimum and maximum x marking the footprint. On 3d maps a minimum and maximum y are also taken, marking the rectangle of ground the ramp covers.

Bottom height. The height of the ramp's low end, where the climb starts. Defaults to 0, ground level. The high end's height follows from the slope, so you only set the bottom.

Slope amount. How many whole levels of height the player gains or loses for each tile they move along the ramp. Defaults to 1, the gentlest slope, one level per tile. Higher numbers climb faster; it can't be less than 1.

Direction. Which way the ramp climbs, read as walk this way to go up, with the opposite walk going down.

Bottom surface (a platform tile, or none). The sloped floor the ramp lays under the player as they climb, so they have ground the whole way up. Pick none to lay that floor yourself.

Top surface (a platform tile, or none). A flat landing set at the high end, so stepping off the top of the ramp gives the player ground to stand on. Pick none to build the landing yourself.

Each surface also takes its own volume and pitch. Walls are always yours to build: if the player could walk off a side or edge, add a wall there yourself, since a slant never makes walls.

Auditioning sounds in the builder.

While a surface tile list is focused in the form, press space to hear the highlighted tile's step clip, hold control and press L to hear its land clip, and hold control and press H to hear its fall clip.

Authoring slants by hand.

If you edit main.sif directly, a slant on a 2d map is written as slant minx maxx base direction slope bottomtile bottomvolume bottompitch toptile topvolume toppitch. On a 3d map a minimum and maximum y are inserted after maxx: slant minx maxx miny maxy base direction slope bottomtile bottomvolume bottompitch toptile topvolume toppitch. Base is the low end's height and the top height is worked out from the slope, so it isn't stored. The direction is written with an underscore, such as right_up or left_down. A surface's tile is the word none to leave it out.
