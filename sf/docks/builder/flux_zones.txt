Flux zones.

A flux zone is an invisible area that gradually changes your stats the more you move through it. Each step you take across its ground nudges your walk speed, health, stamina, or shield up or down by a set amount, and the change sticks with you after you leave — so the longer you walk it, the more it builds. It's the tool for ground that wears you down as you cross it, a runway that winds your speed up the farther you go, or a cursed patch that quickens your step while it bleeds you.

How a flux zone works.

A flux zone covers a rectangular area, and every time you take a step onto its ground it adjusts your stats by the amounts you set, once per step. Standing still does nothing — it only acts when you actually move, so how much it changes you depends entirely on how far you walk across it. Each of the four stats is independent: a single step can raise one while lowering another, or touch only the ones you set and leave the rest alone.

The change is permanent. Unlike ground that only affects you while you stand on it, a flux zone moves your real stat and the new value stays with you when you step off — your speed keeps whatever it climbed to, and drained health or stamina is really gone until you recover it the usual way. Dying resets everything back to normal.

Each amount can be positive or negative. A positive number raises that stat a little with each step, a negative number lowers it, and zero leaves it untouched. A flux zone with every amount left at zero does nothing at all.

The surface setting decides which ground the zone acts on. Left at any, it affects every tile inside the zone; set to a specific platform tile, only that kind of ground triggers the changes, so you can lay a zone over a mixed floor and have it act on only the ice, say. When flux zones overlap, the one built later wins on any stat they both set, so you can carve a stronger or gentler pocket inside a larger zone. A flux zone is silent — your normal footsteps play as you cross it.

How a flux zone differs from heal zones and tile zones.

Two other elements feel similar, so it helps to know the line between them.

A heal zone changes your health and stamina while you stand still, ticking away on a timer. A flux zone changes them only as you move, one nudge per step, and it reaches your speed and shield too, not just health and stamina.

A tile zone sets a property while you're standing on it and snaps it back the instant you step off. A flux zone never snaps back — each step permanently moves your real stat, and it stays that way after you leave.

In short: a heal zone is standing and time, a flux zone is walking and steps, and a tile zone is a temporary override where a flux zone is a permanent build-up.

Flux zone fields.

The flux zone form takes the following:

Coordinates. A minimum and maximum x and a minimum and maximum y (and a minimum and maximum z on 3d maps), marking the area the zone covers.

Speed step. How much your walk speed changes with each step on the zone's ground. Positive raises it, negative lowers it, zero leaves it alone. There is no upper limit, and it will never drop below 1, the slowest you can move.

Health step. How much your health changes each step. Positive heals a little per step, negative wounds a little per step. A draining health step can wear you all the way down and kill you, the same as any other damage.

Stamina step. How much your stamina changes each step. Positive restores it, negative drains it.

Shield step. How much your shield's strength changes each step, but only while you have a shield raised. Positive repairs it, negative wears it down, and draining it to nothing breaks the shield. With no shield up, this setting does nothing.

Surface. Which ground the zone acts on, chosen from the platform tiles with any at the top. Any affects every tile in the zone; a specific tile affects only that kind of ground.

Authoring flux zones by hand.

If you edit main.sif directly, a flux zone on a 2d or topdown map is: flux_zone minx maxx miny maxy surface speed_step health_step stamina_step shield_step. On a 3d map a minimum and maximum z follow the y pair: flux_zone minx maxx miny maxy minz maxz surface speed_step health_step stamina_step shield_step. The surface is a platform tile name or the word any, and each of the four steps is a whole number — positive to raise that stat, negative to lower it, or zero to leave it alone.
