Camera markers.

The camera is a free-moving viewpoint you can send around the map on its own, without moving your character. Its main jobs are to let you look at what is out there and to drop selection markers - saved coordinates that the build forms fill in for you when you place something, so you rarely have to read off and type numbers by hand.

Entering camera mode.

There are two ways to take control of the camera:
Hold the camera key (g by default) for a momentary look. While it is held the camera detaches from you and your movement keys drive it instead of your character; let go and the camera snaps straight back to you.
Toggle the dexterity camera on (y by default) to keep the camera active hands free, so you do not have to hold anything. It announces dexterity camera enabled, your movement keys drive the camera until you toggle it back off, and then control returns to your character.

Moving the camera.

While you are in camera mode your ordinary movement keys move the camera one tile at a time instead of your character - left and right, forward and back or up and down, and page up and page down to change height on a 3d map. The camera stops at the edges of the map.

Setting selection markers.

Move the camera to a spot and press a marker key to save that position as a selection point. The four markers are left, right, back, and front, set with j, l, k, and i while you are in camera mode; on a flat 2d map the back and front keys set bottom and top instead, and on a 3d map shift plus k and shift plus i set the bottom and top markers on the height axis. Each one you set plays a confirmation sound and speaks its value. A marker that is already set is not overwritten - pressing its key just reads back the value it already holds - so clear it first if you want to move it.

What the markers are for.

The selection points you set become the starting coordinates the build forms hand you. Mark the corners of the area you want to build in, open the builder for whatever you are placing, and its coordinate fields arrive already filled with your markers, ready to accept or adjust. This is the quickest way to place something exactly where the camera was without measuring by hand.

Viewing and clearing markers.

The view markers key (semicolon by default) speaks every selection point you currently have set, or tells you there are none. The clear markers key (apostrophe by default) wipes them all so you can start a fresh selection.

Describing a spot.

The describe key (r by default) speaks what is at the camera's current position: the floor tile there, any hazard on it, whether your character is standing on that spot, the coordinates, and how far the spot sits from you - right, left, in front, behind, above, or below, with the distance. It is a quick way to read the map around the camera without setting anything.

Keeping a spot in your ear.

Two more camera tools help you read the map by sound. Move focus with camera (m by default) keeps the camera's tile sound centered in your head no matter how far you aim the camera, instead of letting it fade into the distance as the camera travels away, so a far-off tile stays as easy to hear as a near one; toggle it back off to hear the tile from its real position again. The playback key (t by default) plays the tile sound at the camera's current spot, positioned relative to where you are standing, which is handy for fixing a location in your memory before you move on.

Sharing keys with the sonar.

The j, l, k, and i keys are shared with the sonar's direction keys. When you are in camera mode the marker behavior wins; the rest of the time the same press aims the sonar. See the controls topic for the full list of camera keys and how to rebind any of them.

The arena.

Camera markers are turned off inside the arcade arena, since there is no building there.
