Security jets.

A security jet is an enemy plane that hunts the player along the ground and takes to the air whenever it is struck. It rolls across the map homing in on the player, and when it reaches them it rams them for damage; the moment the player lands a hit on it, it roars up into the air and cruises back and forth high overhead before dropping back down to resume the chase. The player wears it down with their weapons over several passes — easiest while it is on the ground, harder while it is flying — until its health runs out. Security jets only exist on 2d and 3d maps, since a topdown map has no height to fly at.

How a security jet works.

A security jet begins on the ground and homes toward the player, moving one tile at a time and looping its ground sound so the player can hear it coming. It rolls straight through walls, so cover is no defence — the only ways to deal with it are to hit it or to keep out of its reach. When it catches up to the player's tile it strikes them for its attack value, and it keeps striking on its fire time for as long as it is on top of them. It cannot strike a player who is flying a glider, driving a vehicle, or riding a bike.

The instant the player lands a weapon hit on a grounded jet, it takes off. It climbs to its flight height and then patrols back and forth across the full width of the map, turning around at the edges; on a 3d map it roams both across and along the map, criss-crossing the whole area overhead. While it is up there it plays its engine sound and is much harder to hit, since it is moving fast and is no longer coming to the player. After its flight time is up it descends, touches down, and goes back to hunting along the ground. Each time it is hit on the ground it flies again, so bringing one down is a matter of landing hit after hit across its passes.

Destroying a security jet.

A security jet has health and is destroyed by the player's weapons — bullets, melee, and a glider's ramming charge all wound it, and it sounds a hurt cue with each blow and a death cue when its health is gone. It is easiest to hit while it is rolling on the ground, so that is the moment to pour on damage; a hit there also sends it flying, so a fast weapon can chase it into the air and keep chipping at it. Bringing a jet down rewards the player with experience, scaled by the jet's level, and announces the defeat in the combat log.

Security jet fields.

The security jet form takes the following:

Coordinates. A starting x and y (and z on 3d maps) — the ground tile where the jet begins and returns to when it lands.

Health. How much damage the jet can absorb before it is destroyed. Defaults to 1.
Attack. How much damage the jet deals to the player each time it rams them on the ground.
Fire time. Milliseconds between rams while the jet is on the player's tile, so a smaller number is a more punishing squeeze.
Speed. Milliseconds between each one-tile step, governing both the ground chase and the air patrol, so a smaller number is a faster jet.

Level. A reward multiplier for destroying the jet. When it is shot down the experience earned is its xp times its level (times the player's experience rate), and the combat log names the level it fell at. Defaults to 1.
XP. The base experience earned for destroying the jet, before it is multiplied by the level and the experience rate. Leave it at zero, the default, for a jet that gives no reward.

Flight height. How high the jet climbs when it takes off, given as a height on the map. It is capped at the map's ceiling, so a value taller than the map simply flies at the top. Defaults to 5.
Flight time. Milliseconds the jet cruises in the air before it comes back down to the ground. Defaults to 10000.

Security jet sound (a folder name from the security jet sounds list, or none). The audio theme; the bundle supplies the ground loop, the flying engine loop, and the takeoff, landing, ram, hurt, and death cues. Pick none for a silent jet.

Auditioning sounds in the builder.

While the security jet sound list is focused in the form, press space to hear the hurt cue of the highlighted theme, and hold control and press a letter to hear one of its other clips: L for the ground loop, E for the flying engine loop, I for the takeoff, H for the ram, N for the landing, and D for the death.

Authoring security jets by hand.

If you edit main.sif directly, a security jet is: jet x y health attack firetime speed level xp flightheight flighttime jettype. On 3d maps a starting z follows the starting y: jet x y z health attack firetime speed level xp flightheight flighttime jettype. Note that the keyword on the map line is jet, not security jet. The security jet sound must match a security jet sounds folder name or the word none.
